SWRD Defect Insertion Trial

Six real SWRD defects, one per class. Each is cut out of its own film by removing it, then inserted into a defect-free SWRD film. Flip between the original film, the physical insertion and the naive insertion at the same spot.

How a defect is moved

  1. Remove. Inpainting fills a hidden part of an image using the pixels around it. The labelled defect is hidden and OpenCV's Telea method fills it from the border inward, each new pixel a weighted average of the known pixels next to it. Real film grain copied from just beside the defect is then added, so the filled area is not suspiciously smooth.
  2. Subtract. Original minus removed leaves only what the defect did to the film: how many grey levels darker each pixel became. Everywhere else the difference is zero, so what remains is the defect on its own, like a shadow.
  3. Add. On a defect-free film, find a spot at the same position across the weld bead and add the shadow there. It is first scaled by how grainy the host is compared with the source, so it stands out from the grain as much as it did originally. The label polygon moves by the same offset, so the mask is exact.

Why adding is the right operation. A defect is missing steel. On SWRD, missing steel lowers the grey value by the same amount whether the film around it is bright or dark; the pore-dip test measured this. That is Mery and Katsaggelos's additive model. Their log step is not needed here, because film already responds to the log of the X-ray dose.

The naive arm skips steps 2 and 3. It pastes the source pixels themselves, including the brightness of the source film around the defect, which is why it shows up as a blob.

What to judge on each defect

  1. Would you flag the physical insertion as fake?
  2. Is the position on the weld plausible for this class?
  3. Does its darkness sit right against the host film's grain?

Technical details

  • Removal is Felix's idea from 7 Oct: the inpainted area covers the label grown by 6 px, and the grain refill is the fine texture of the same-size window next to it.
  • Defect alone is smoothed by 1 px and kept to darkening only, because missing steel can only darken. This is the sign constraint of RQ3.
  • Placement compares brightness profiles across the weld, column medians over ±150 rows. The score is the worst of three slices along the defect, so spots next to a brightness step lose.
  • Contrast scale is host grain divided by source grain, each measured as the robust spread of the fine texture within 150 px.
  • Naive arm is the raw source crop alpha-blended with the same soft mask: full weight on the label, fading to zero over 6 px.

Limits of this trial

  • Six examples judged by eye. No detector was trained and no realism study was run.
  • Sources were picked for visibility, at or above the median contrast of their class. That filter must not be reused to build training data.
  • Removal leaves a visible ghost on the inclusion and a smooth band on the large lack of penetration. Good enough to extract a defect, not yet good enough to make clean hosts.
  • The extracted defect still carries some source grain. A proper noise model belongs to RQ3.
  • Grain-ratio scaling assumes both films share film type and gradient.
  • The naive arm is a pure alpha blend. A brightness-matched or Poisson paste is a stronger control to decide on before the WP4 A/B.
